01

How it works

Can we spot delivery problems early enough to prevent them — and choose a response that costs less than the expected failure? OrderGuard answers it in four steps.

simulate

Run a marketplace

Merchants, drivers, customers and orders, each order tracked through preparation, driver assignment, travel and its final outcome.

seed · duration · supply · demand
score

Detect risk

Every event re-scores the order 0–100 from observable factors like merchant backlog, driver reliability, customer reachability and projected lateness.

event-reactive, not polled
decide

Price the response

Each intervention is compared against doing nothing on expected cost: direct cost plus the residual cost of failure.

min(direct + residual)
measure

Compare strategies

The same seeded scenario runs three ways, so outcomes and intervention spend can be compared like-for-like.

none · threshold · expected-value
02

Ground truth vs. what the engine can see

The simulator decides what actually goes wrong. The risk engine has to infer it — the same way a real operations system would.

Failure hazard rates are the simulator's hidden ground truth. The risk engine never receives these values directly — it must infer risk from observable marketplace state.

That separation keeps the evaluation honest: the model can't score well by peeking at the answer.

Expected-value reasoning

OrderGuard's engine doesn't act just because risk is high. It picks the option with the lowest total expected cost — and "do nothing" is always on the table. Drag the inputs to see the choice change.

One seed, three strategies

The Simulation Lab runs the same seeded marketplace three ways and reports real, measured outcomes from an actual simulation run. Nothing is precomputed.

Threshold-based

If an order's risk score crosses a fixed trigger, act. Simple — but it spends the same way on every flagged order.

if score ≥ 50: intervene

Expected-value engine

No fixed threshold. For each order, choose the action with the lowest direct cost plus residual expected failure cost.

argmin(direct + P(fail|a) × cost)

06

Scope & limitations

A working simulation and decision-support prototype — scoped deliberately, and labelled honestly throughout the product.

By design
  • ✓Fully syntheticAll merchants, drivers, customers and orders are generated. No real platform, drivers or customers are contacted.
  • ✓Explicit rules, not MLRisk scoring uses transparent rules, so every score can be broken down factor by factor.
  • ✓Measured vs. estimatedTimelines are badged measured; scores and recommendations are badged model estimate.
  • ✓Assumptions are assumptionsIntervention costs and effects are modelling inputs, and one simulation doesn't establish real-world effectiveness.
Out of scope
  • —AuthenticationA public demo, open to anyone exploring it.
  • —Live marketplace integrationsNo connection to a real delivery platform or dispatch system.
  • —Large-scale distributed processingBuilt to run a city-sized simulation clearly, not millions of orders.
