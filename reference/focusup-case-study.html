Overview

The problem with “just focus”

Students don’t lack study time — they lose it. A two-hour block becomes forty minutes of real work between notifications, tab-switching and deciding what to do next. Most timer apps solve the clock but not the friction: they don’t know what you’re working on, and they give you nothing when you finish.

FocusUp treats a study session as a small ritual with a beginning, a middle and a reward. It asks what you’re working on, protects the time while you’re in it, and makes the payoff visible when you’re done — so the next session is easier to start.

How might wehelp students start focused work faster, stay in it longer, and actually feel the progress they make?

Design goals

Three principles shaped every screen

Start in seconds

Every extra decision is a chance to procrastinate. The primary action is always the biggest thing on screen, and smart defaults do the rest.

→ Home, Set up

Protect the zone

Once a session starts, the interface steps back: one timer, one motivating line, and only the controls you might need.

→ Focus

Make progress felt

Finishing should feel like a win. Streaks, focus points and a completed task turn an invisible habit into something you can see grow.

→ Complete, Home

Key screens

Screen by screen

01 · Onboarding

Start with intent, not a sign-up form

The first question the app asks is the one that matters most: what are you focusing on today? Answering it sets up the rest of the experience before the user has typed a single thing.

  • Horizontal carousel picker — Exams, Assignments, Notes and more slide past a centred, highlighted chip. It’s faster than a list and feels playful on first open.
  • Headline as motivation — “It’s time to FocusUp” doubles as brand moment and nudge, with a single glowing CTA.
02 · Home

Your week at a glance, one button to begin

Home answers two questions instantly: how am I doing, and what’s next? A personal greeting and weekly total lead into a single, oversized “Start Focus Session” button.

  • Week strip keeps today anchored in context without a full calendar view.
  • Today’s Activity pairs a time-goal pill with a task list showing estimated minutes, so planning and doing live side by side.
  • Centre play button in the tab bar makes starting a session reachable from anywhere in the app.
03 · Session setup

Three choices, then you’re in

Setup is deliberately short: what you’re working on, how long, and how protected you want to be. Everything else is optional.

  • Duration presets (25, 50, 90 min) reflect common study rhythms, with Custom for everyone else.
  • Environment toggles — focus music, silenced notifications, focus mode — turn the phone from distraction into ally.
  • Optional goal field gives the session a finish line without forcing extra input.
04 · Focus session

A calm timer that stays out of the way

During a session the interface quiets down. A glowing progress ring and large numerals carry the screen, readable at a glance from across a desk.

  • Progress ring shows elapsed time spatially, so you feel how far you’ve come, not just the minutes left.
  • Motivational line — “Discipline today creates freedom tomorrow” — sits above the timer as a quiet reminder of why.
  • Today’s Tasks stay in reach at the bottom, so checking something off never means leaving the session.
05 · Session complete

Celebrate the finish, set up the next start

The reward screen is where the habit is built. It names the user, names the achievement, and turns one session into visible progress.

  • Four stat tiles — goals completed, focus time, current streak, focus points — give different kinds of motivation to different kinds of students.
  • Completed task card connects the session back to real work.
  • “Take a break” leads, with “Back to home” secondary — encouraging healthy rest instead of burnout.
Visual system

Designed for late-night study

Students often study in the evening, so FocusUp uses a deep midnight palette that’s easy on the eyes in a dark room. A periwinkle-to-aqua gradient marks every primary action, giving the app a single, recognisable signal for “go.”

Reflection

What I learned, and where it goes next

Takeaways

  • Hierarchy is behaviour design. Making “Start” the largest element on every screen did more for the goal than any feature.
  • Rewards need restraint. The complete screen celebrates without gamifying so hard that the work itself gets lost.
  • A consistent signal builds trust. Reserving the gradient for primary actions made each screen instantly readable.

Next steps

  • Usability testing with students during exam season to validate session lengths and the setup flow.
  • Accessibility pass on the smallest labels and captions, checking contrast against the dark gradient.
  • Stats & streaks tab to expand the weekly view into long-term progress and study patterns.
